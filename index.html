AI Governance Dashboard

A one-page brief, one prototype screen, and the data plan behind it. All numbers on the screen are made up.

Framing

Who this is for and what it must do

The problem

Teams buy and use AI tools on their own. Nobody can answer, with confidence, how much the company spends on AI, who is near a budget limit, or which tools are in use that IT never approved.

Primary user

The head of IT or IT operations who owns the AI budget and gets asked "what are we spending on AI?" every month. The CFO is the second reader and gets the same screen as a monthly summary. I picked one primary user so the screen can be simple. This is a bet to validate in interviews.

Assumptions

  • A spend limit is a monthly dollar amount per team, set by finance with the team lead. Per-tool limits come later.
  • Company of 500 to 5,000 people, with a mix of approved tools and tools people found themselves.
  • Version 1 reports and alerts. It does not block anything.
  • Only counts, dates and cost are collected. Prompt and response content never is.

Not in version 1

  • Blocking tools or cutting off spend at a limit.
  • Ranking or naming individual employees.
  • Charging costs back to teams.
  • Judging whether AI use is productive.

One headline number, one alert strip, three charts. Each chart is titled with the question it answers. Everything else is deliberately left off (list under the screen).

      Part 3

      Why these three charts

      I worked backwards: the decision the user needs to make, the question that informs it, the chart that answers it, then the data it needs.

      Spend against budget

      Question
      Will we stay inside budget?
      Decision
      Raise the budget, tighten limits, or do nothing.
      Why a line
      A total hides direction. The forecast shows the day the budget is crossed while there is still time to act.
      Data
      Vendor cost APIs, budget upload.

      Spend against limit, by team

      Question
      Who is near a limit?
      Decision
      Raise a limit, ask a team to cut back, or require approval.
      Why bars
      Scaling each bar to its own limit makes a $3k team and a $15k team comparable. Sorted by risk, the problem teams are at the top.
      Data
      Cost APIs, identity provider for team mapping, limits.

      Active users by tool

      Question
      How much use is outside approved tools?
      Decision
      Approve it, offer an approved replacement, or block it.
      Why users
      Free and personal tools show no spend, so people are the only honest measure of shadow AI.
      Data
      Sign-in logs, network data, expense feeds.

      Left off on purpose: a pie of spend by tool (rarely leads to an action), token counts (engineers care, a CFO cannot act on them), and a ranked list of individual users (invites blame and raises privacy concerns).

      Scoping

      MVP, success metrics and risks

      MVP, then later

      1. MVP: identity provider, usage APIs for the 3 or 4 biggest tools, budget upload. Charts 1 and 2 in full, chart 3 from sign-in data only.
      2. Next: expense feeds and network discovery, which make chart 3 reliable.
      3. Later: gateway and cloud billing, then optional enforcement.

      How we would know it works

      • Share of AI spend attributed to a team. Proposed target: 90% or more.
      • Limit breaches flagged before they happen, not after month end.
      • Days to spot a new unapproved tool.
      • Finance and IT opening it weekly.
      • Check against noise: share of alerts dismissed as not useful.

      Biggest risks

      • Attribution. Shared API keys hide who spent what. Needs one key per team.
      • Coverage. Some tools expose no data, so each figure carries an "exact", "estimated" or "detected only" tag.
      • Trust. Employees may see it as surveillance. Show teams by default, keep individuals for admins.

      Where it fits among existing products

      SaaS management tools such as Torii, Zylo and Productiv track app spend broadly. Security tools such as Netskope, Zscaler and Microsoft Purview detect AI traffic. Each vendor's admin console shows only its own tool. The gap I would target is one AI-specific view across vendors that ties spend to team limits. I would check each competitor's current AI features before claiming this gap.

      What I would ask in week one

      • Interview 5 IT leads and 3 finance leads: who owns this today and what do they use?
      • What does "limit" mean in your company, and who sets it?
      • Which three AI tools cost the most, and do they have usage APIs?
      • Would you want alerts only, or the ability to block?
